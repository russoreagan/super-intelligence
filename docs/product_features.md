## A · A relationship, not a tool
*The features that make working with it feel personal and human. Most of the product lives here.*

### Character & mood

> **Consistent core, living mood.** Every persona is built the way a person is.
> **Traits stay constant**: a defined *temperament* (its emotional disposition)
> and *cognitive style* (how it thinks and approaches problems). On top of that,
> its **mood moves in the moment**, and **some of that mood settles in for the
> long run**, shaped by your relationship. A stable character, a mood that swings
> with the moment, and a settled way of being around the people it knows well.

- **Defined traits keep it consistent.** Each persona has a set temperament and cognitive style that don't drift turn to turn. They're its identity, so it always feels like the same someone.
- **A mood that shifts in the moment.** On top of those traits, its mood moves the way a person's does. A good result lifts it, friction weighs on it, and that colors the very next thing it does.
- **A relationship that builds over time.** Some of that mood *persists*: how it generally feels around you (at ease, energized, guarded) accumulates across your whole history together instead of resetting each session. That slow, earned drift is what makes each agent one of a kind, never a copy of you.
- **Reads your emotional context.** It picks up tone, urgency, and mood in what you say and adjusts: more careful when it matters, lighter when it doesn't.
- **A real internal state, not a mask.** Its focus and mood genuinely shift with what's happening and steer how it attends, decides, and remembers — not a friendly tone bolted on at the end.
- **You can see how it feels.** The app's color shifts with its mood in real time. A calm task and a tense one don't look the same. Its inner state is ambient in the interface, not buried in logs.
- **Tune its temperament.** Start from distinct personalities and adjust traits to fit the role: how cautious, how curious, how expressive.

### Memory that makes it personal

- **Perfect recall.** Past conversations, decisions, and results are retrievable precisely. Nothing important is lost.
- **Memory you can read and edit.** Its understanding lives in plain, human-readable notes you can open, correct, and own. No black box.
- **Knows the people it works with.** It builds an understanding of who it's talking to over time, how you communicate, and what you care about, so it stops feeling like starting over.
- **Remembers conclusions, not just chats.** It carries forward what was actually decided and found, and picks back up on open threads.

### Voice with feeling

- **Natural voice, both ways.** Talk to it and hear it back in a natural voice.
- **Emotion in the delivery.** The voice carries the mood of the moment, so it sounds present rather than flat.
- **Recognizes who's speaking.** It can tell voices apart and adapt to the person in the room.
- **Your choice of voice.** Pick the voice that fits each personality.

## B · Real learning, kept honest
*How it actually learns, and the guardrails that keep learning from making it lazy or unsafe.*

### How learning happens

- **Gets better at your work over time.** It reinforces the approaches that produced good outcomes and drops the ones that didn't, so its judgment sharpens with use. It doesn't just accumulate notes; it changes how it decides.
- **Turns repetition into practiced routines.** Work it does again and again is consolidated into a compact, replayable form. It's the difference between following a recipe and knowing how to cook.
- **Transfers what it learns across problems.** It remembers not just *what* happened but *how* it approached it, so a brand-new problem with a familiar shape can borrow a strategy that worked somewhere else.
- **Consolidates while it rests.** During downtime it reviews recent work, extracts durable lessons, and updates what it knows about you, so it starts fresh from what it learned, not from a longer transcript.

### How learning is enforced (the guardrails)

- **Shortcuts that audit themselves.** Every learned routine is continuously checked against full reasoning and benched the moment it stops matching reality. Nothing goes stale silently.
- **The things that never get shortcut.** Requests to *act* are always fully understood before anything runs; budgets and approvals never relax with repetition; emotionally charged moments always get full attention. Run 100 faces the same checks as run 1.
- **Earned instincts.** Its sense of what's worth doing only pays out for genuine, uncertain wins, so it can't inflate its own confidence by repeating what's easy.

## C · Fits how you work
*Connect it, operate it, and build with it on your terms.*

### Two ways to run it

- **Drop it into your product.** The full engine is available as an API, so you can build the agent directly into your own app, workflow, or product. Uniquely, it returns the agent's emotional state alongside every response, so your product can react to *how it's feeling*, not just what it said.
- **…or run it from the Elyceum app.** A complete operator experience ships with it; there's no front end to build. Everything below works out of the box.

### Operate it from the app

- **Build agents without writing code.** Create personalities, give them roles and bounded permissions, connect their tools, and choose their skills and voice, all from a visual builder.
- **Monitor the whole system at a glance.** Watch live activity, autonomous jobs, spending, and what each agent is learning, all in one place. Alerts and approvals surface the moment something needs you.
- **Step in whenever you want.** Jump straight into any agent's conversation to take over, answer an approval, or steer it. Real human-in-the-loop control, not just read-only dashboards.
- **An Admin agent that watches over the system for you.** A built-in operator agent helps you keep an eye on health, activity, and cost. It's a second set of eyes on everything: just *ask it* what's going on instead of piecing it together from charts.

### Connect and extend

- **Connect your tools and data.** Plug in the services and data sources it should work with.
- **A deep library of reasoning skills.** It draws on a large, curated set of problem-solving approaches and picks the right one for each task.
- **A team of agents, each with a role.** Run multiple personalities with defined jobs and bounded permissions.
- **Bring your own models and providers.** Works with your choice of AI providers and your own keys. You're not locked in.
- **Your keys, your data.** Bring your own provider keys; your memory and your data stay yours, and its reasoning stays inspectable.

## D · Better with experience
*It delivers real work, and it gets better and cheaper at it the more you use it.*

### Delivers real work

- **Runs multi-step jobs on its own.** It plans, executes the steps, and reports back while you're away. Outcomes, not just answers.
- **Reflects between conversations.** In idle time it keeps thinking, surfacing follow-ups, connections, and things worth your attention.

### Improves with use

- **Gets cheaper the more you use it.** It recognizes work it's done before and replays it instead of reasoning from scratch, so your cost-per-outcome falls with use. That's the opposite of a memory file that gets slower and pricier as it grows.
- **Fast on routine, full effort on the novel.** Familiar work is streamlined; a genuinely new or unusual problem automatically gets full attention.
- **Better results as it gains experience.** It starts each run from what the last one actually found, and builds on strategies that have worked, so quality compounds over weeks, not just within a session.
- **Watch your cost-per-outcome fall.** A live view shows the trend as the agent practices. The savings are visible, not a promise.

## Before you publish: verify, don't overclaim

- **"Reflects between conversations"** (idle thinking) is powerful but is **off by default** in the hosted build. Enable it, or soften the claim, before featuring it.
- **"Recognizes who's speaking"** (speaker recognition) exists in the engine. Confirm it's wired up in the hosted app before promising it.
- **The compounding claims** ("gets cheaper / better with experience," "transfers across problems") are real and live, but they *emerge with use*. Phrase them as what happens over weeks so a day-1 evaluator isn't disappointed.
