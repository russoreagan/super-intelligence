# Day Trading Framework — Analysis

_Generated 2026-06-05 22:57. Advisory framework: the system flags and analyzes setups; the human keeps all final decisions. This is not a stock-picking or auto-execution system._

## 1. Capital Allocation

- **Risk-first, not allocation-first.** Position size is derived from a fixed per-trade risk budget rather than from a target dollar exposure.
- **Per-trade risk = 1% of account.** On a $5,000 account that is **$50 of risk per trade** — the most you accept losing if the stop is hit.
- Capital is deployed opportunistically against qualified setups, not pre-committed in fixed buckets. The sector-balance rule (below) governs *how* deployed capital is spread, not a static cash split.
- Keep capital in instruments liquid enough to exit fast; intraday, unrealized exposure should never exceed what the 1% stop can defend.

## 2. Position Sizing

- **Formula:** shares = (account risk budget) ÷ (entry − stop distance per share). With a $50 risk budget, a $0.01–$0.025 stop distance implies roughly **2,000–5,000 shares**.
- This is the working share range cited for the $5,000 account — it follows directly from tight stops on low-priced, liquid names, not from a desire to trade large size.
- **Wider stop ⇒ fewer shares.** The 1% cap is the constant; share count flexes to keep risk fixed across setups.
- Every position must have a defined stop *before* entry — sizing is meaningless without it.

## 3. Sector Balance

Target spread of active exposure, designed to **avoid correlation risk** (so a single sector move doesn't sink the whole book):

| Sleeve | Weight |
|---|---|
| Growth tech | 40% |
| Financials or industrials | 30% |
| Consumer | 20% |
| Defensive | 10% |

- Growth tech carries the largest weight because it supplies the volatility day trading depends on; the financials/industrials and consumer sleeves diversify the driver; the defensive sleeve dampens beta.
- Treat these as ceilings/targets for concurrent exposure, not quotas to fill every session.

## 4. Stock Types to Look For

Screen for tradability, not narrative:

- **Intraday volatility** — enough daily range to produce profit beyond costs.
- **Volume / liquidity** — heavy, consistent volume so size can be entered and exited without slippage.
- **Tight bid–ask spreads** — low friction per round trip; wide spreads quietly tax every trade and break the tight-stop math.

These three together are what make the 2,000–5,000 share / 1%-risk model viable.

## 5. Common Intraday Setups

Grounded in the framework's market-analysis toolkit (MAs, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands; flags, pennants, double tops/bottoms, head-and-shoulders):

- **Opening-range breakout** — mark the first N minutes' high/low; trade the break with the trend, stop on the opposite side of the range.
- **VWAP reclaim / rejection** — VWAP as the intraday line in the sand; longs above on reclaim, shorts on rejection.
- **Trend-continuation flags/pennants** — enter the consolidation breakout in the direction of the prevailing move.
- **Reversal patterns** — double top/bottom and head-and-shoulders at exhaustion, confirmed by RSI divergence.
- **Momentum/indicator confirmation** — MACD cross and Bollinger Band expansion to time entries and avoid chop.

## Operating Notes

- **Human-in-the-loop.** AI flags setups and logs every decision — including **veto and gut-override reasons** when the user passes on or overrides a trade — to build a feedback log for learning. Capturing *why* a trade was skipped is treated as as valuable as the trade itself.
- **Live feedback over backtests.** Preference is real-time, read-only data feeds and live decision logging; historical backtesting is considered insufficient for real-world adaptation.
- **Scope honesty.** The system provides analytical support and verification, not genuine stock-picking edge or trade execution.
